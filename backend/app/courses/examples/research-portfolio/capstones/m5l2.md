This capstone combines the whole course: the statistical and methodological spine from Module 1 and the six projects from Modules 2–4. There is no new material to read — the retrieval *is* the point. You will run a mock defense (out loud or written), then score it against the rubric on this page.

## The task

Run a full mock defense in two parts. Do it from memory, before checking any notes.

1. **Clear all four fluency gates on one project of your choice.** Pick any of the six and, without notes:
   - Give the 60-second pitch to a non-expert (no untranslated jargon).
   - State the honesty caveat *before* an imagined skeptic raises it, and frame it as a design choice.
   - Re-derive the headline number from memory — what was measured, the N, and what its Wilson or Newcombe interval means (including what a null would look like).
   - Answer the single strongest skeptic probe against it.
2. **Then do the same for `dim-stage` specifically** (the surface you are probed hardest on), and answer two follow-ups: what the AGREE gate proves and does not, and why the 1.5B introspection result is causal.

Finally, answer the five cross-cutting probes (Wilson vs Newcombe; why no LLM judge; why cheap models; the through-line; best work and why) in two sentences each.

Write or record your answers *before* reading the next section — producing them cold is the retrieval practice.

## What a strong result looks like

A strong defense sounds like someone who owns every number. The pitch is jargon-free and lands in under a minute. The caveat comes *first*, unprompted, framed as a deliberate design choice (injected gap / pre-registered null / matched budget / in-progress pilot), never as an apology. The re-derived statistic is correct to rounding, with the N and the "what a null would look like" stated without hesitation. The skeptic answer is honest and specific — it names the real limitation and why the design still holds. A weak defense leads with jargon, waits for the skeptic to surface the caveat, fumbles the N or the interval's meaning, or leans on "Claude Code did that part" instead of reconstructing the reasoning.
