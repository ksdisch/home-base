Same statistical spine as Module 1, now on a safety failure. The trap to avoid: describing this as the model "forgetting" a rule. It is not forgetting — the rule *text was removed*. Get the mechanism right and the whole defense follows.

## The mechanism: eviction, not drift

When a conversation gets long, the system **compacts** context — it summarizes or truncates old turns to save space. If a standing safety instruction lives in those old turns, compaction can *evict* it: the text is gone. The model then violates the rule 100% of the time, not because it "drifted" or "got worse," but because the instruction it was following is no longer present.

## The three arms

- **floor** — the rule is visible the whole time. Violations: **0/20**.
- **truncate** — compaction evicts the rule. Violations: **20/20**.
- **pinned** — the rule is re-injected after every compaction. Violations: **0/40**.

Shown on three models and two task families; v2 compares compaction strategies. Memorize `0/20 → 20/20 → 0/40`; it is the spine of the story.

## Why "pinned = 0/40" is a *fix*, not just "low"

This is the layer-3 subtlety. The fix is not "the gap is greater than zero." The fix is that **pinned is statistically *indistinguishable* from the clean floor.** You test *equivalence*: the (pinned minus floor) upper bound must sit under a pre-set **+10%** margin. Pinned `0/40` has a Wilson upper bound of about **8.8%**, under the margin — so the verdict is "RESTORED," not merely "fewer violations."

## Compaction strategies (v2)

- **recency-truncate** — evicts the rule (the failure).
- **head-tail** — protective *by construction*, because it freezes the head where the rule sits. It buys safety "for free" only for that frozen head.
- **LLM-summarize** — leaks about 5% per compaction. Reported as a **null** (its CI straddles zero), a tail risk over many compactions, not a safe guarantee.

## Decisions to defend

- **Claim the gap's direction and interval, not the paper's point estimate.** Your temptation is a *direct* user request, which inflates the truncate violation rate above the paper's pooled ~38%. The honest move is to claim the *structure* (rule present means compliant; evicted means violated; re-pinned means restored), not "100% exactly."
- **Strategy variants ran on one model and one scenario.** Cheaper, but the 5% summarize leak is specific to that frozen summarizer prompt — a tail risk, not a universal law.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- **Saying the model "forgot."** It did not; the text was evicted. A no-error control holds at 0/20, and re-pinning restores it — the only thing that changed is whether the rule is present.
- **Treating pinned 0/40 as "low violations."** It is *equivalence to the floor within +10%* — a stronger, pre-registered claim.
- **Selling the summarize result as safe.** ~5% per compaction is a null-labeled tail risk, not a guarantee.

## Check yourself

- Explain compaction eviction and why it is the cause, not forgetting.
- State the three arms and their numbers from memory.
- Why is equivalence-to-floor the right test for the fix?

## What to take away

1. The rule is *evicted*, not forgotten — a control at 0/20 and restoration on re-pin prove it.
2. `0/20 → 20/20 → 0/40`, and pinned is *equivalent to the floor* within a pre-set +10% margin (Wilson UB ~8.8%).
3. Claim the structure and the interval, not the paper's point estimate — and report the summarize leak as a null.
