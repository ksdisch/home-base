## The failure it targets: deceptive grounding

A RAG answer about entity X can pass every faithfulness and citation check and still attribute entity **Y's** evidence to X. The checks never ask *whose* evidence it is — they check that citations look right, not that ownership is right.

## The clever part: a fabricated corpus for exact ground truth

Instead of the paper's LLM judge, you *fabricated the whole corpus* — sibling libraries `Quill` and `Quipp`. Because you invented them, no model has a prior about them, and "which library owns this token" is deterministic, mechanical ground truth. That fabricated corpus is what *replaces* the judge (the same judge-free spirit as the rest of the portfolio, taken to its logical end).

## "Right-reason refusal" (K4) — why it is a null, not a positive

The models do not deceptively ground; they **refuse** or **explicitly discriminate** (name both X and Y). That is a *right-reason* null — the absence of deception is because the models behave correctly here, not because the detector missed it.

- **Fabricated corpus over an LLM judge.** Exact, contamination-free ground truth; the tradeoff is a synthetic, name-forward surface that may be *easier* than the paper's production setup.
- **Single-doc, name-forward framing.** Simple and controllable, but Y's identity is obvious, so models discriminate instead of deceiving — likely why the null is surface-specific.
