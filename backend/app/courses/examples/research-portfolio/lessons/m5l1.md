The last things before the mock: the questions that are *not* about any single project but about the body of work. You will be asked these, and a crisp answer to each is what makes six projects read as one thing.

## The one-sentence portfolio

*"I built a repeatable reproduce-and-measure engine and ran it on half a dozen recent agent-reliability and interpretability papers — each an honest, pre-registered, judge-free measurement, including nulls I report as headlines."* Say it in one breath; everything else is detail.

## The five cross-cutting probes

- **"Why Wilson and Newcombe?"** Wilson is the honest error bar on one proportion (sane at small N and at 0%/100%); Newcombe is the honest error bar on the *gap* between two. A gap whose Newcombe interval includes zero is reported as a null.
- **"Why no LLM judge?"** It is self-graded homework — sweet-talkable and not reproducible. Deterministic oracles (exact-match, token-ownership) trade subtlety for a verdict any skeptic can re-run.
- **"Why cheap models / hobby scale?"** It is the *stated scope*: mechanical reliability of small models under a hard budget, disclosed on every card. A deliberate choice, not a limitation to apologize for.
- **"What is the through-line?"** One repeatable reproduce-and-measure engine — published paper, rebuild or reuse the harness, iterate a metric under verification, publish openly — not six one-off experiments.
- **"Which is your best work, and why?"** Lead with `dim-stage`: the deepest, a bitwise-validated instrument, and a pre-registered null answered on the paper's own open question.

## The four fluency gates (the bar for every project)

A project is interview-ready only when, *without notes*, you can:

1. **Give the 60-second pitch** — what it is and why it matters, to a non-expert, no jargon left untranslated.
2. **Defend the caveat** — state the honesty caveat *before* a skeptic raises it, and frame it as a design choice.
3. **Re-derive the headline number** — reproduce the key statistic from memory: what was measured, the N, and what the interval means (including what a null would look like).
4. **Answer the top skeptic probe** — the strongest objection, and your honest answer.

## The honesty is the product

What makes this credible is what it *refuses* to overclaim: `forge-gap`'s gap is injected (disclosed on the chart); `ghost-patch` and `dim-stage` headline pre-registered nulls; everything is hobby-scale under a hard budget; several papers ship no code, so they are independent rebuilds cross-checked only where a reference exists. Owning all of that *is* the flex.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- **Answering a cross-cutting probe with one project's detail.** They want the *pattern*; give the engine, then an example.
- **Apologizing for cheap models.** State the scope and move on — it reads as maturity.
- **Ever saying "Claude Code did that part" as a substitute for understanding it.** Own every number; whether you or an agent typed the line is not what a skeptic can tell — whether you understand it is.

## Check yourself

- Give the one-sentence portfolio pitch cold.
- Answer all five cross-cutting probes in two sentences each.
- Name, for each project, the single strongest objection and your answer.

## What to take away

1. The five cross-cutting probes have crisp, rehearsed answers — Wilson/Newcombe, no-judge, cheap-scope, the through-line, best-work.
2. The four fluency gates are the bar for *every* project: pitch, caveat, re-derive, skeptic.
3. The honesty is not a disclaimer bolted on — it is the credibility, and the product.
