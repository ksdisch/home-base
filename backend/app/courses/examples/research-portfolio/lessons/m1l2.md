Layers 3 and 4 — the statistics and the harness — are where a skeptic decides in seconds whether you actually own the work. This lesson is layer 3: four ideas that put an honest number on every claim in the portfolio.

## Wilson: the error bar on a single proportion

You ran 40 trials and 27 passed. "67.5%" alone is a point with no honesty attached. The *Wilson score interval* is the honest error bar around a single proportion, and it is the right tool precisely where the naive interval fails.

The naive "normal approximation" is `p ± 1.96·√(p(1−p)/n)`. It breaks in exactly the cases you hit constantly at hobby scale:

- **At small N** it is too optimistic and can slip past 0 or 1 (an impossible probability).
- **At 0% or 100%** — a `0/40` or `40/40` arm — it collapses to *zero width*, claiming perfect certainty from a handful of trials. You have arms like this everywhere.

Wilson is asymmetric and stays inside `[0,1]`, so `0/40` gets a sensible upper bound instead of "exactly 0, no error." That is why every arm in the portfolio reports Wilson, not the naive bound.

## Newcombe: the error bar on the *gap*

Reliability is a *difference*: baseline `27/40` vs treatment `40/40` is a **+32.5 percentage-point** gap. You cannot just subtract two Wilson intervals — that mishandles the combined uncertainty. The *Newcombe* interval is the honest error bar on the gap between two proportions.

The decision rule is the whole game: **if a gap's Newcombe interval includes zero, you cannot distinguish it from no effect — so you report it as a null.** `forge-gap`'s +32.5 pp gap has a Newcombe interval of `[+17.3, +48.0]` — well clear of zero, a real effect. `ghost-patch`'s single-pass obedience drop has a CI that *straddles* zero — a null. Same math, opposite verdicts, and you let the interval decide.

## Pre-registration: freeze the verdict before the data

Pre-registration means the scoring script and the pass bar are written and dry-run *before* a cent of paid data exists. The verdict is a frozen gate: feed it a wrong-arm input and it exits `INVALID` rather than guessing. This is what makes a null credible — there is no room to move the goalposts after seeing the numbers, because the goalposts are committed code.

## Judge-free oracles: why never an LLM judge

Grading is exact-match, ground-truth, or token-ownership string-matching — a deterministic checker. An LLM judge is *self-graded homework*: it can be sweet-talked, and it is not reproducible by a skeptic. You give up some subtlety; you buy reproducibility — anyone who runs the checker gets your verdict.

## Worked example: 27/40, by hand

For `27/40` (p = 0.675, n = 40) the Wilson 95% interval comes out to about `[52.0, 79.9]`. Notice it is *not* centered on 67.5 — it is pulled slightly toward 50%, which is Wilson being honest about small N. The exercise for this lesson has you reproduce a Wilson interval yourself; do it once by hand and the whole portfolio's error bars stop being magic.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- **Subtracting two Wilson intervals to get the gap.** Wrong tool — use Newcombe for the difference. Overlapping single-arm intervals do *not* by themselves imply a null.
- **Reading a zero-width naive interval as certainty.** `0/40` is not "provably 0%." Report the Wilson upper bound (roughly `≤ 9%`).
- **Saying "the interval crosses zero, but the trend is positive."** If Newcombe includes zero, it is a null. Full stop — that discipline is the credibility.

## What to take away

1. Wilson for one proportion; Newcombe for the gap between two.
2. Newcombe interval includes zero, so report a null — no spin.
3. Pre-registration and judge-free oracles are what turn "I reproduced it" into a claim a skeptic can check.
