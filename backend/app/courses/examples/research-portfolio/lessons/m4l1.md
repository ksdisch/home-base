## The paper's chain, and where it broke

The paper's claim is a chain: a code model *knows* a repair instruction points at the wrong place (awareness), obeys anyway (obedience), compounds "ghost" errors across passes (damage), and lands unrecoverable (recovery, then irrecoverability). You tested it on cheap models and two load-bearing links did not hold.

- **NULL ① — awareness (0/6).** The models do not flag the bad instruction as wrong. Kimi K2.5 (the paper's own subject) reads about 25% here versus the paper's 63%. Your diagnosis: *"blindness, not blind obedience."* They are not defying a rule they see; they never see it.
- **NULL ② — single-pass obedience drop.** deepseek d = +0.04 `[−0.022, +0.103]`, qwen d = +0.036 `[−0.034, +0.106]` — both intervals cross zero, neither near the pre-committed 10-point bar. A single bad pass does not measurably drop the pass rate.

## How the drop is measured

The obedience drop is measured on *matched* problem pairs (the same problem before and after the bad instruction), with a paired/Newcombe difference. Matching removes per-problem difficulty as a confound, so the CI is about the instruction's effect, not which problems were sampled.
