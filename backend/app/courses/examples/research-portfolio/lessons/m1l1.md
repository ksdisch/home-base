Every reproduction study fights the same suspicion: *did you just tune things until the result looked good?* This portfolio answers that suspicion structurally. It runs one small, disciplined loop — the same five steps on every paper — and each step exists to close a specific way reproductions usually cheat.

## The five-point loop

1. **Pick a recent failure-mode paper.** Days-to-weeks old, usually unreplicated and shipping *no code*. Rebuilding a claim with no reference implementation is harder — and more credible — than re-running someone's repo. Each paper names a *primitive*: a specific way a model breaks (a rule decays, a memory welds in an error, a citation check goes blind).
2. **Reproduce a narrow slice on cheap models, at hobby scale.** Small open models (GLM, DeepSeek, Qwen, Qwen2.5 0.5–3B) via OpenRouter, under a hard budget guard (typically under $5, tracked to the cent). The scope is *mechanical reliability of small models under a controlled test* — not frontier capability, and that scope is disclosed everywhere.
3. **Pre-commit the statistics.** The scoring script is written and dry-run *before* the paid data exists. Margins and expected nulls are pre-registered. Every arm gets a Wilson interval; every gap between arms gets a Newcombe interval. A wrong-arm input makes the verdict script exit `INVALID` — the gate is code, not a judgment call made after seeing the numbers.
4. **Grade with judge-free, deterministic oracles.** Exact-match, ground-truth, or token-ownership string-matching — never an LLM judge. A machine that cannot be sweet-talked decides pass/fail, so anyone who runs the checker gets the same verdict.
5. **State the narrow, honest delta and build in public.** Every prompt, filter, model swap, and gate is logged in a `DECISIONS.md`. The framing is always *"reproduced and measured — here is my honest delta,"* never *"I invented this."* A null is a reportable headline, not a miss to hide.

## Why each step is load-bearing

The loop removes the three classic ways a reproduction cheats:

- **Self-graded homework** is killed by step 4 (no LLM judge).
- **Moving the goalposts** is killed by step 3 (pre-registered statistics).
- **Hiding the misses** is killed by step 5 (nulls are headlines).

Take those away and "I reproduced it" stops being a vibe and becomes a checkable claim.

## Worked example: reading the loop on one project

`decay-pin` reproduces "an in-context safety rule silently decays when compaction evicts it." Step 1: a recent no-code paper naming the primitive (constraint decay). Step 2: three cheap models, two task families, under budget. Step 3: pre-registered arms (floor / truncate / pinned) with an equivalence margin fixed in advance. Step 4: a deterministic violation check, not a judge. Step 5: the numbers (0/20 then 20/20 then 0/40) reported with intervals, and the one strategy that only leaked ~5% reported as a *null*, not spun. Same five steps you will see on all six.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- **Calling this "just re-running papers."** Several targets ship no code — these are independent rebuilds, cross-checked against a reference only where one exists. That is the harder, more credible path.
- **Treating "cheap models" as a weakness.** It is the *stated scope*. Owning it ("mechanical reliability of small models under a hard budget") reads as maturity; apologizing for it invents a gap.
- **Thinking a null means the project failed.** A pre-registered, judge-free null is a *measured result*. Two projects here headline nulls on purpose.

## What to take away

1. One loop, five steps, run many times — that repeatability *is* the portfolio's thesis.
2. Each step maps to a specific anti-cheat: judge-free, pre-registered, nulls-as-headlines.
3. Learn this loop once and you have the spine of all six projects.
