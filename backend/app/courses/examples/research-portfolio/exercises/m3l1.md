## Problem

1. The pinned arm is 0/40. Compute the Wilson 95% upper bound and confirm it is about 8.8%. Explain why that number (vs the +10% margin) yields the verdict "RESTORED."
2. Truncate is 20/20, floor is 0/20. Sanity-check that the +100 pp gap has a Newcombe lower bound well above zero (the repo reports `[+77.2, +100]`).
3. One sentence: why is "equivalence to the floor" a *different and stronger* claim than "the gap is greater than zero"?

## Solution

1. For 0/40, the Wilson upper bound is about `z^2 / (n + z^2)` = `3.84 / 43.84` = about `0.088`, i.e. **8.8%**. Since 8.8% is under the pre-set +10% equivalence margin, the pinned arm is statistically indistinguishable from a clean floor: "RESTORED." If the UB had exceeded 10%, you could not claim equivalence.
2. 20/20 vs 0/20 is a +100 pp gap. Even with only 20 trials per arm, the Newcombe lower bound sits well above zero (`[+77.2, +100]`) — the eviction effect is unambiguous, not a small wobble.
3. "Gap greater than 0" only says truncate is worse than pinned. "Equivalent to floor" says pinned is *as good as never losing the rule at all* — a positive claim about the fix, bounded by a margin fixed before the data. The second is what lets you say "re-pinning restores compliance," not just "helps."
