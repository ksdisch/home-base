## Problem

1. M1 deepseek obedience drop: d = +0.0400 on 150 clean pairs, 95% CI about `[−0.0218, +0.1029]`. In two sentences, explain why an interval straddling zero means "indistinguishable from no effect."
2. The pre-committed bar was a 10-point (0.10) drop. Does d = +0.04 with that CI clear the bar? What do you conclude?
3. Contrast with the *paper's* claimed drop: what would the CI have to look like to count as a reproduction?

## Solution

1. The CI `[−0.0218, +0.1029]` contains 0, and even negative values — so the data are consistent with the bad instruction causing *no* drop (or a tiny improvement). You cannot distinguish +0.04 from 0 at this N; by the pre-registered rule, that is a null.
2. No. The point estimate (0.04) is below the 0.10 bar, and the interval does not even exclude zero, let alone reach 0.10. Conclusion: at the cheap tier, a single bad pass does not measurably reduce the pass rate — a pre-registered null.
3. To count as a reproduction, the drop's CI would need to sit *entirely above zero*, ideally at or beyond the +0.10 bar. Instead it straddles zero — which is why this is reported as a null, not a weak positive. The honest read: the paper's effect likely needs a stronger model or the iterative regime, not one cheap-tier pass.
