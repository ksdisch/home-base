## Problem

Reproduce forge-gap's headline and one natural gap.

1. Flagship: baseline 27/40, treatment 40/40. Compute both Wilson 95% intervals (confirm about `[52.0, 79.9]` and `[91.2, 100]`) and the Newcombe interval on the +32.5 pp gap (confirm about `[+17.3, +48.0]`).
2. Natural gap: llama-8b 0/40 to 18/40. Confirm the gap is +45.0 pp with a Newcombe interval of about `[+28.2, +60.2]`.
3. One sentence: which of these two gaps is "injected," and why that label changes what you may claim.

## Solution

1. Wilson(27/40) is about `[52.0, 79.9]`. Wilson(40/40) is a 100% arm, where the naive interval would be zero-width; Wilson gives about `[91.2, 100]`. The Newcombe interval on 40/40 − 27/40 = +32.5 pp is about `[+17.3, +48.0]`; it clears zero, so the recovery effect is real (on the injected fault).
2. 18/40 − 0/40 = +45.0 pp; Newcombe about `[+28.2, +60.2]`, also clearing zero — a real natural gap on the weaker model.
3. The flagship +32.5 pp is *injected*: the fault was seeded, so you may claim "error-recovery closes a controlled recovery gap," not "GLM-4.6 naturally fails 32% of the time." The +45 pp llama-8b gap is natural, so it *does* support "weak models genuinely fail here, and a guardrail fixes it."
