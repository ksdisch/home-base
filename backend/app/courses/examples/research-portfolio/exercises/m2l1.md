## Problem

The dim-stage introspection signal (1.5B): α=8 gives 30/101 reportable, the α=0 control gives 0/101.

1. Compute the Wilson 95% interval for 30/101. Confirm it is about `[.217, .392]`.
2. The α=0 control is exactly 0/101. Argue in two sentences why that makes the introspection effect *causal* rather than correlational.
3. Contrast: what would you conclude if the control had been, say, 25/101 instead of 0/101?

## Solution

1. p = 30/101 = about 0.297, n = 101, z = 1.96. Wilson center is about 0.304, half is about 0.088, giving about `[0.217, 0.392]`. It clears zero comfortably, so reportability at α=8 is a real signal, not noise.
2. The only thing changed between the arms is the steering dose. With the dose off (α=0) reportability is *exactly* zero — the thought is not spontaneously readable — and turning the dose on produces it. A manipulated cause with an inert control moving the outcome from 0 to about 30% is the definition of a causal effect, not a correlation.
3. If the control were 25/101, most "reportability" would exist without any steering — the effect would be mostly baseline, and you could not attribute it to the intervention. The control at exactly 0 is what buys the causal claim; that is why it is the number to lead with.
