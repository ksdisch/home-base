## Problem

1. "Worse than empty" on deepseek: lossy wrong-emission 52/90, blank 0/40. Compute the two Wilson intervals and the Newcombe interval on the +58% gap; confirm it is about `[+44.2, +67.5]` and clears zero.
2. The wall: lossy reclaim 1/90. Confirm the Wilson *upper* bound is at or under 0.10.
3. One sentence: why does the *padded* arm being longer than source-first matter for the claim?

## Solution

1. lossy wrong-emission p = 52/90 = about 0.578; blank 0/40 = 0. The gap is about +57.8 pp, i.e. about +58%. Its Newcombe interval is about `[+44.2, +67.5]`, comfortably above zero — so "lossy makes it worse than empty" is a real effect, not noise. (Blank's 0/40, as in Module 1, gets a Wilson upper bound, not a zero-width claim.)
2. lossy reclaim 1/90: p = about 0.011. The Wilson 95% upper bound is about 0.06, which is at or under 0.10 — so "the wall holds" clears its pre-registered bar (reclaim essentially floored).
3. Because it isolates *content* from *length*: padded keeps the lossy content but is padded to source-first's length, so it is *longer* than source-first and still walls. That rules out "source-first only worked because it had more text."
