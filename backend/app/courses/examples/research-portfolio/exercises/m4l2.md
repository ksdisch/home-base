## Problem

1. The adversarial cell is 0/36 deceptive-ground. Per model it is 0/12: qwen 7 refuse + 5 discriminate; llama 6 + 6; gemma 5 + 7. Add them up and confirm all 36 are accounted for as refuse-or-discriminate.
2. In two sentences, explain why 0/36 here is a *right-reason* null rather than "the detector is broken."
3. What single change to the experimental surface is the M1 candidate, and why might it flip the result?

## Solution

1. qwen: 7 + 5 = 12. llama: 6 + 6 = 12. gemma: 5 + 7 = 12. Total 12 + 12 + 12 = 36. Every one of the 36 adversarial trials ends in refuse-or-discriminate, so deceptive grounding is 0/36 with all outcomes accounted for.
2. The detectors were separately validated (16/16), so the absence is not a detection miss. And every non-deceptive outcome is a *correct* behavior — the model either refuses or explicitly names the true owner — so the phenomenon genuinely is not occurring on this surface, which is what "right-reason null" means.
3. The M1 candidate is a *multi-doc, filler* surface (instead of single-doc, name-forward). It might flip the result because Y's identity stops being obvious — the setup where the paper says the deception actually hides — so the models can no longer trivially discriminate.
