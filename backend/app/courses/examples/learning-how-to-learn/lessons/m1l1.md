# The Forgetting Curve

In the 1880s, Hermann Ebbinghaus memorized lists of nonsense syllables and tested himself over
time. He found something uncomfortable but useful: **forgetting is fast and predictable.**
Without any reinforcement, memory for new material drops sharply within hours and keeps decaying
over days. This decay shape is the *forgetting curve*.

## The key idea

Most of what you "learn" in a single sitting is gone within a week unless you do something
about it. The thing that fights the curve isn't studying *harder* in one block — it's
**revisiting the material at the right times**.

Each time you successfully recall something, the curve gets *flatter*: the next drop is slower.
Review isn't repetition for its own sake; each review resets and lengthens the interval before
you'd forget again.

## Why timing beats volume

- Re-reading the same chapter five times today produces one shallow, quickly-decaying trace.
- Recalling it once today, once in two days, once next week produces a durable memory — for
  *less* total time on task.

This is the single most leveraged insight in the whole course: **distribute your reviews over
time instead of massing them together.** The later lessons turn this into a concrete schedule.

## What to take away

1. Forgetting is the default. Plan for it instead of being surprised by it.
2. The fix is *spaced* review, not *more* review.
3. Recalling beats re-reading — which is exactly what the next lesson is about.
