# Encoding vs. Retrieval

Learning has two distinct acts, and confusing them is the most common study mistake.

- **Encoding** is getting information *in* — reading, watching, highlighting, taking notes.
- **Retrieval** is getting it *back out* — recalling it without looking.

## The re-reading trap

Re-reading is pure encoding. It feels productive because the text becomes *familiar*, and we
mistake familiarity for knowledge. But recognizing a sentence when you see it is not the same as
being able to produce the idea when you need it. This is why you can re-read a chapter, feel
confident, then blank on the exam.

> Fluency illusion: the smoother material feels to read, the more we overestimate how well we
> know it.

## Desirable difficulty

Robert Bjork's research shows that conditions which make learning feel *harder and slower* often
make it *stick better*. Struggling to recall something — even failing and then checking — builds
a stronger memory than effortlessly re-reading it. These are **desirable difficulties**:

- Recalling from memory instead of looking it up.
- Spacing reviews so you've partly forgotten (the next lesson).
- Mixing topics so you can't coast on autopilot (interleaving).

The discomfort is the signal that real learning is happening. If studying always feels easy,
you're probably just encoding.

## What to take away

1. Familiarity ≠ knowledge. Test whether you can *produce* it, not just recognize it.
2. Effortful retrieval is a feature, not a bug.
3. Build study sessions around recalling, not re-reading.
