# Spacing & Interleaving

Two scheduling techniques do most of the heavy lifting. Neither is about *what* you study —
both are about *when* and *in what order*.

## Spacing (distributed practice)

Spread your reviews out over time instead of cramming them together. The same total study time
produces dramatically better retention when distributed.

A simple **expanding-interval** schedule for something new:

1. Review shortly after first learning it (same day).
2. Again after ~2 days.
3. Again after ~1 week.
4. Again after ~2–4 weeks.

Each successful recall lets you push the next interval out further. (This is exactly what the
Learning Hub's spaced-repetition "Review next" queue automates for your quizzes.)

## Interleaving

Instead of practicing one skill in a long block (AAAA then BBBB), mix them (ABCABC). Blocked
practice feels smoother and produces better *immediate* performance — which is why it's tempting
— but interleaving produces better *retention and transfer*.

Why it works: mixing forces you to repeatedly **choose which approach applies**, which is the
actual skill you need later. Blocked practice lets you switch to autopilot.

### When interleaving helps — and when it doesn't

- **Helps** when items are confusable and you must learn to tell them apart (math problem types,
  similar concepts, languages).
- **Less useful** for the very first exposure to one isolated skill, where some blocking helps
  you build the basic motion before mixing.

## What to take away

1. Distribute reviews on expanding intervals; let success stretch the gaps.
2. Mix related topics rather than blocking one at a time.
3. Expect interleaving to *feel* worse and *work* better — that's the desirable difficulty again.
