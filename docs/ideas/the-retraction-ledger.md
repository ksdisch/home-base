# The Retraction Ledger — the brief that grades what it got wrong, and lets that change tomorrow's sweep

**Status:** Idea — not committed. Added by `/replenish` (Moonshot lane) on 2026-08-12.
**Note:** Captured under the moonshot-lane freeze; build only after the ~08-19 verdict.

_A nightly resolver re-checks Home Base's own archived factual claims against the world a week after it made them, opens each morning with the ones that came back revised or wrong, and accumulates a per-source reliability record that — once it has earned a graded week — feeds back into the sweep prompt to demote domains that keep getting it wrong._

## Premise

Home Base's zero-fabrication bar is enforced entirely at write time: sourced, dated, deduped, exclusion held to the same bar as inclusion — and then the claim is never looked at again. The archive is 31 day-folders (2026-07-13 → 2026-08-12), 163 topic files, 574 items and 1,442 source links across 318 distinct domains, all of it frozen assertions nobody has graded. The one grading lane that shipped, Calibrated Doubt, grades a rare opt-in directional wager on whether a story keeps moving — 116 resolutions, 29 hits, Brier 0.406 over 17 graded mornings — by intersecting normalized headline/URL identity keys (`_item_identity_keys` in `backend/app/sweeps.py`). It is structurally incapable of noticing that a number, a date, or a framing in a digest was wrong. The Retraction Ledger measures accuracy retroactively instead of asserting it, and lets the measurement change behavior: Kyle reads "I told you X on July 30; the revised figure is Y," and a domain that missed three of five lab-release numbers gets named in tomorrow's sweep preamble. The habit bar stops resting on 'never fabricated' and starts resting on 'corrects itself in public' — which means the product can now lose, on the record, in front of its only user.

**Why now:** The human antibody the project shipped for exactly this risk has already lapsed, and it is checkable: `docs/sweep-trust-log.md` contains exactly one entry — the 2026-07-19 M0 baseline — 24 days ago against its own 30-day "re-grade due" threshold, so the monthly manual re-grade has never once happened and the amber warning fires in six days. Meanwhile the 2026-08-12 brief itself carries the trajectory argument as a sourced item: Anthropic now invisibly watermarks all Claude text output worldwide with C2PA-signed provenance on images (The Decoder / Forbes / The Register / Euronews, August 11) — the open web is moving toward majority-synthetic, and "I found a source" stops being evidence of truth. A brief whose whole trust story is write-time sourcing is on the wrong side of that curve. The archive is also at the right size right now: 574 items is enough corpus for a real backfill and small enough that a one-time walk is affordable. Timing constraint, stated honestly: the moonshot lane is frozen until the ~08-19 v1 verdict, so this is captured now and built after.

## The bet

The one thing that must be true: a web-searching resolver can rule held / revised / wrong / unresolvable on Home Base's own archived claims accurately enough that Kyle believes the corrections — because a single false retraction costs more trust than the error it claims to fix, and the abstention rule that protects the sweeps (`unresolvable` must be the cheap, common, unembarrassing default) has to survive being pointed at the product's own past. A veteran of this project flinches twice: first because `docs/ideas/sweep-trust-warranty.md` explicitly put "any automated/LLM-driven accuracy scorer" out of scope as a gated new surface, and this is exactly that scorer, built deliberately; and second because the source-reliability record eventually rewrites the preamble at `sweep.sh:150` — the first time Home Base's own output changes because a model judged the product wrong. Everything else here is ordinary: one user, local files, one grounded `claude -p` lane, no new acting surface, nothing sent anywhere.

## Decisions / open questions

- A false retraction is the fatal failure mode, worse than the original error — does `unresolvable` genuinely stay the cheap default, or does a judge asked "was this right?" drift toward manufacturing findings to look useful? Needs an adversarial dry-run on items known to be correct before any of it reaches the page.
- D-7 is a guess at the revision half-life. Sports scores settle in hours; lab-release numbers and funding figures get revised over weeks. Per-topic resolution windows, or one global D-7 for v0?
- Does the per-source reliability record survive small-N honestly? 318 distinct domains over 574 items means most have 1–3 items, and a demotion computed off n=2 is noise wearing a number. Minimum-sample floor, or restrict demotion to the ~12 domains with 25+ items (cnbc 107, espn 67, si 50, insidethehall 50, …)?
- Should the source record be visible to Kyle at all, or only to the prompt? A domain leaderboard is the most legible artifact here and also the most damaging if it's wrong.
- Is Kyle's own "actually, that was right" override a first-class verb? Supersede-by-append supports it for free; the question is whether the moonshot is weaker without a human veto or weaker with one.
- Cost and lane: one batch call per day-of-archive (bounded, cheap) or one per topic (better grounding, ~8x)? `sweep.sh`'s subscription-lane guard applies either way.
- Sequencing: the moonshot lane is frozen until the ~08-19 v1 verdict (soul assumption 6) — this is captured, not scheduled.

## Credible first step

Build the resolver as a strict clone of the Overnight precedent, which is the same shape end to end. New `sweeps/resolve_claims.py`, modeled on `sweeps/actions_queue.py` (363 lines: scrubbed `_LANE_ENV_VARS` child env, empty scratch cwd, closed stdin, strict-out validation, per-day idempotency + `--force`, cost rows via `sweeps/envelope.py`). Scope the nightly increment to D-7 only — the ~18 items from one week ago, not the whole archive — and offer the model only items carrying a checkable quantitative or dated assertion; every other item resolves `unresolvable` for free with no call. Run it in the sweeps' own web-enabled lane (`--allowedTools "WebSearch" "WebFetch"`, as `sweep.sh:180` does), because resolving forward genuinely requires looking at the world again, with the sourcing bar on a `revised`/`wrong` verdict identical to the bar on inclusion: no primary source, no verdict. Append validated rows to `backend/data/claim-resolutions.jsonl` — jsonl, not a `claim_resolutions` SQLite table, because every peer grading ledger in this repo is append-only jsonl in `backend/data` and because `calibration.jsonl`'s supersede-by-append + last-per-key collapse (`_read_ledger`, `backend/app/sweeps.py:463`) is exactly the property a retraction that itself gets retracted needs; `schema.py` stays at v14 with no migration. Chain it best-effort at the tail of `sweep.sh` beside `deliver_brief.py`. Serve it with a new `backend/app/corrections.py` (modeled on `backend/app/overnight.py`, 111 lines) → `BriefCorrections`/`BriefCorrection` in `backend/app/models.py` beside `BriefCalibration:914` → `BriefResponse:961` → the live-only block in `get_brief` (`backend/app/api/brief.py:195–228`, gated `if date is None and served` like mirror/readiness/calibration/overnight, so an archived `?date=` morning stays a record and never re-grades). Render a "Corrections" strip at the top of `frontend/src/pages/Brief.tsx` following the "Yesterday's calls" strip at :809, with `frontend/src/api/types.ts` kept in sync (`api-types-sync`). Backfill the 31-day archive once behind a flag, and hold the source-demotion feedback into `sweep.sh:150` OFF behind the same `trial` gate Calibrated Doubt uses (7 distinct graded mornings) — the judge does not steer the sweeps until its own record has been checked.

## Dependencies

The archive at `data/sweeps/<date>/<topic>.json` (verified: 31 days 07-13→08-12, 163 topic files, 574 items, 1,442 source links, 318 domains) · `sweeps/actions_queue.py` as the guarded-lane template + `sweeps/envelope.py` for cost rows · `sweep.sh`'s best-effort tail chain and its `preamble=` construction at :150 · `backend/app/config.py`'s ledger-path convention (`brief_calibration_ledger`:92, `brief_overnight_ledger`:97) · the live-only serve gate and `BriefResponse` in `backend/app/api/brief.py` / `models.py` · `frontend/src/pages/Brief.tsx` + `types.ts` (via the `api-types-sync` skill) · Calibrated Doubt's `trial` gate as the borrowed honesty mechanism · a working Claude subscription lane with web tools. Blocked until the ~08-19 verdict lifts the moonshot freeze.

## Explicitly out of scope (revisit later)

No automatic source *removal* or topic pausing — demotion is prompt language, never a silent roster edit. No rewriting or deleting anything under `data/sweeps/` (the archive stays immutable; corrections live in the ledger). No re-grading of an archived `?date=` morning — it is a record, not a grader. No touching the existing wager/Brier lane, its ledger, or its strip; the Retraction Ledger sits beside Calibrated Doubt, not on top of it. No emailed/iMessaged/audio corrections in v0 — web strip only. No new acting surface, no sends, no writes outside `backend/data/`. No replacing the manual `docs/sweep-trust-log.md` re-grade; this makes it cheaper, it does not certify itself. No SQLite table and no schema migration.

## Identity/positioning note

none — tethered. Still one user's morning brief, local files, one grounded lane, no new acting surface, and it deepens rather than replaces the zero-fabrication premise. The honest caveat, same shape as `calibrated-doubt.md`'s: it does not change what the project IS, it flips the trust principle underneath it — from a product that has never been caught wrong to a product that publishes its own error rate.

## What it changes

The trust premise moves from write-time abstention to a maintained record. Today `data/sweeps/` is append-only truth-by-assertion; after this, every claim has a forward-resolution state and the brief opens by naming its own misses before it names the news. Tomorrow's sweep prompt stops being frozen text plus a date preamble and becomes text plus a date plus an earned source record — the first feedback loop in the project where yesterday's measured performance changes today's generation. `GET /api/brief` gains a live-only `corrections` block; the Today page gains a strip above the topics; `backend/data/` gains one more append-only ledger; nightly sweep cost rises by one bounded batch call (default sonnet, like `OVERNIGHT_MODEL`). What does not change: one user, local files, phone as thin client, no acting surface, nothing sent or executed, sidecars read-only.
