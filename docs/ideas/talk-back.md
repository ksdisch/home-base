# Talk Back — the morning brief becomes an interruptible conversation you have out loud

**Status:** Idea — not committed. Added by `/replenish` (Moonshot lane) on 2026-08-12.
**Note:** Convergence: FOUR blind lenses (futurist · constraint-killer · first-principles · emotional-core) independently proposed this move — the strongest convergence of the run. Captured under the moonshot-lane freeze; build only after the ~08-19 verdict.

_While the morning MP3 plays on the phone, press-and-hold and speak: the narration ducks, local Whisper transcribes, the question is answered aloud in the same Kokoro voice about the exact item that was playing, and "note that…" utterances land as real `brief_notes` rows on that item — then playback resumes at the playhead._

## Premise

Home Base's moment of peak presence isn't the screen — it's earbuds in, walking, the brief in your ear — and it is the one moment the product is completely deaf. Two numbers say how deaf: `backend/data/brief-chat.jsonl` holds **exactly one row** since the "Ask about this" lane shipped on 2026-07-16, and `brief_notes` holds **three notes, total, ever** (07-23, 07-28, 08-03) against the habit bar's ≥3 *per week*. Both surfaces demand a screen and a keyboard at the exact minute Kyle has a phone in one hand and coffee in the other, so the richest signal the system could collect — what he actually says about a story — evaporates on the walk. Talk Back makes that channel two-way: speech becomes a read path (barge in, get a grounded answer about the item that was literally playing) and, more importantly, a **write path** (the mutter becomes an item-anchored note without a tap). Every piece it needs is already installed and running: `com.voicemode.whisper` on `:2022` exposing an OpenAI-compatible `/v1/audio/transcriptions`, `com.voicemode.kokoro` on `:8880` that `render()` already POSTs to, `tailscale serve` fronting the hub over real HTTPS (the secure context iOS demands before it will hand a page a microphone), and the scrubbed no-web-tools `claude -p` lane in `backend/app/chat.py`. The premise shift is that the brief stops being a document that was produced and becomes a session that happened.

**Why now:** The extended v1 verdict lands ~2026-08-19 and the notes criterion is the leg that failed the 08-03 check — not marginally, by ~15x. The typed follow-up lane has now had four weeks to prove the screen is the right place to ask a question and it has one row to show for it, which is the cleanest possible evidence that the surface, not the appetite, is what's broken. Everything the idea depends on already runs on this Mac (Whisper `:2022`, Kokoro `:8880`, HTTPS via `tailscale serve`), so the cost is design courage rather than infrastructure. And the moonshot lane is frozen under D7 until that verdict — which makes now exactly the right time to *capture* this at full strength and exactly the wrong time to build it.

## The bet

**The bet:** Kyle already reacts out loud during the walk, and the reason those reactions never become notes or questions is *modality*, not absence of thought — so a microphone converts an existing behavior into the system's scarcest input. If that's true, the notes leg (3 lifetime vs ≥3/week — a ~15x gap no amount of UI polish closes) gets fed by something Kyle does anyway. If it's false, the mic records five mornings of silence and the honest verdict is that notes are a *want* problem — which is exactly why this gets prototyped and judged rather than shipped.

**What makes a veteran of this project flinch:** (1) a hot microphone inside a resolutely trust-first, local-first product that has never accepted an input that wasn't a tap or a keystroke; (2) latency — the one real measurement in the ledger is **11.0 seconds** for a `claude -p` round trip, before TTS, and eleven seconds of silence after a barge-in is not a conversation, it's a hang; (3) the honesty problem, hardest of all — a spoken answer has no visible citation, so the sourcing bar has to be *said out loud* ("one source, Reuters, from yesterday's sweep") in the exact modality where fabrication is least checkable; (4) a mis-transcription now reaches into the store, and the repo's precedent for anything that writes (Overnight = draft-only, Calendar = opt-in removable ledger) says speech must be gated the same way; (5) a project built on deterministic renders and files on disk grows a real-time session and a mic permission.

## Decisions / open questions

- **Latency is the design problem, not a tuning problem.** The only real measurement is 11.0s for a `claude -p` answer, before TTS. Decide the turn-taking model before building: instant local acknowledgment + "keep walking, I'll cut in when I have it" (answer arrives asynchronously at a natural segment boundary) versus duck-and-wait. The former is probably the honest design and is a bigger idea than it looks.
- **What must the voice say to keep the sourcing bar?** Proposal: every spoken answer opens with the item's own attribution ("From the Reuters item in this morning's AI brief…") and explicitly says "nothing newer than yesterday's sweep" when the question needs freshness. This is the single most soul-critical decision in the idea.
- **Where does the item_id derivation live?** `audio_brief.py` is stdlib-only on system python3 and cannot import `app.sweeps`, so the sha1 formula gets duplicated. Either duplicate it plus a backend test asserting both implementations agree over a fixture day, or have the backend build the offsets→item map at serve time instead. Two silent copies of an id scheme is a real drift hazard.
- **Does a spoken note write, or draft?** The precedent for every acting surface (Overnight = draft-only with approve, Calendar = opt-in removable ledger) says a mis-transcription must not reach the store unreviewed. But a note that needs a tap on Today later re-imports the friction this idea exists to delete. Likely answer: write immediately, but render voice-origin notes with their transcript visible and one-tap discard.
- **Push-to-talk or real barge-in?** PTT is the honest v0 — no hot mic, no wake word, explicit intent. True interruption is what makes it a companion rather than a dictation button. Ship PTT; record that the bet is only half-tested by it.
- **Whisper is running the `base` model.** Word-error rate on windy outdoor phone audio is unknown and could sink the whole thing. Measure it on ten real walk utterances before designing anything downstream of the transcript.
- **Does the utterance audio persist?** Trust-first says discard immediately after transcription; debuggability says keep it. Default to discard; if kept, keep it under `data/` with the same discipline everything else obeys.
- **What does a refuted bet look like on paper?** Define the silence verdict up front so the prototype can actually fail — a moonshot that can only succeed isn't being tested.

## Credible first step

Two halves, both pinned to code that already exists.

**(a) Make the audio addressable.** `sweeps/audio_brief.py:140` `build_script` already returns `tuple[str, list[dict]]` — the ordered segment list beside the script — but its offsets are word-count estimates (`models.py:830`: "a landmark, not a measured timing"; `BriefAudioCard.tsx:166` seeks 2s early to compensate). Render segment-by-segment through the same `render()` Kokoro call, measure each with `ffprobe` (absolute `/opt/homebrew/bin/ffprobe` — the launchd-PATH gotcha is a known trap here), concatenate the frames into `brief.mp3`, and write `brief.segments.json` beside it: `{slug, item_id, start_seconds, end_seconds}` where `item_id = sha1(f"{date}|{slug}|{headline}")[:12]`, the same read-time derivation `backend/app/sweeps.py:176` uses. Fall back to today's single-shot render + word estimate whenever `ffprobe` is absent, so the best-effort-never-fails-a-sweep contract is untouched. This half alone is a strict improvement to a shipped surface.

**(b) Make the player listen.** A push-to-talk control on `frontend/src/components/BriefAudioCard.tsx` — which renders through `BriefShell`'s stable portal, so the mic survives a route hop exactly the way the single `<audio>` element does. MediaRecorder POSTs the clip plus the playhead offset to a new `POST /api/brief/voice` in `backend/app/api/brief.py`, which transcribes against the running whisper.cpp (`http://127.0.0.1:2022/v1/audio/transcriptions`, verified live), resolves the item from the offset against `brief.segments.json`, and routes two ways: a question goes through the existing `build_prompt` + `BriefChatClient` in `backend/app/chat.py` (API key scrubbed, no web tools) and comes back as Kokoro audio from the same `/v1/audio/speech`; a "note that…" utterance goes to `add_brief_note` (`backend/app/store/db.py:372`) with the same field shape `BriefNoteCreate` already uses.

**Smallest judgeable slice:** one real walk — one utterance, one spoken answer that says its source out loud, one note landing on the correct item — with the whole surface degrading to today's exact linear MP3 the moment the STT service is down.

## Dependencies

All verified live on this Mac: `com.voicemode.whisper` LaunchAgent running on `:2022` with an OpenAI-compatible `/v1/audio/transcriptions` route (confirmed from the server's own form action) · `com.voicemode.kokoro` on `:8880`, already the target of `render()` · `tailscale serve --bg` fronting `http://127.0.0.1:8000` at `https://kyles-macbook-pro.tail01200d.ts.net` — **load-bearing**, because iOS will not grant `getUserMedia` outside a secure context, so a plain tailnet-IP URL would kill this idea silently · `ffmpeg`/`ffprobe` at `/opt/homebrew/bin` (not necessarily on launchd's PATH — see the LaunchAgent PATH memory) · the existing scrubbed subscription lane in `backend/app/chat.py` and `add_brief_note` in `backend/app/store/db.py` · **the D7 moonshot freeze** — captured now, built only after the ~2026-08-19 v1 verdict.

## Explicitly out of scope (revisit later)

Wake word or any always-on microphone. Cloud STT, or audio of any kind leaving the tailnet. Multi-turn dialogue memory — every utterance is one grounded turn against one served item, exactly like the typed lane. Voice control of anything that *acts*: triggering sweeps, pausing topics, writing calendar events, approving Overnight proposals — v0 answers and takes notes, nothing more. Desktop or Android microphone support. Speaker diarization or multi-user voice. Replacing or deprecating the typed "Ask about this" surface. Any change to the email/iMessage delivery lane or the MP3's external contract.

## Identity/positioning note

**Not tethered — this is the identity shift, and blunting it would be sharpening it wrong.** Home Base has been, from M0 forward, a producer of artifacts: deterministic renders, files on disk, no live state, a phone that is explicitly a thin client over a Mac that is the single source of truth (load-bearing assumption 3). Talk Back attacks that assumption head-on. It gives the product a real-time session, a microphone permission, and a write path that isn't a keyboard; the interaction's live state originates on the phone, and the Mac becomes an answer service the phone calls rather than the place the product happens. What it deliberately does *not* change is the soul: STT and TTS both local, nothing crosses the tailnet, the answering lane stays the same scrubbed no-web-tools `claude -p`, and the whole surface degrades to today's exact linear MP3 the moment Whisper is down. The identity that shifts is artifact→session and screen→voice. The identity that must not shift is local-first and honest.

## What it changes

The brief stops being a rendered artifact and becomes a session. Speech becomes a first-class write path into the store — the first input this product has ever accepted that isn't a tap or a keystroke. The audio stops being a monologue with estimated landmarks and becomes an addressable, item-anchored timeline whose offsets are measured rather than guessed (which retroactively fixes the FR4 chapter chips). The Mac is demoted from the place the product happens to an answer service the phone calls, with the live state — turn-taking, playhead, the utterance — originating on the phone. And the honesty discipline that currently lives in visible citations has to be re-expressed as something the voice *says*, which is a harder bar, not a softer one.
